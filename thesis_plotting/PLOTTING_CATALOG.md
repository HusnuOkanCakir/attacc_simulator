## Headline figure preview

### F1 — Thesis main claim (§6.5.0a, n=2k)
![fig_e5_gpu_vs_hybrid](figures/fig_e5_gpu_vs_hybrid.png)

Both models, both metrics: **hybrid PIM+GPU beats GPU-only**. Pi0:
+59% throughput, −52% E2E p99. OpenVLA: +26% throughput,
−13% E2E p99. Azure conv at scale=1.0; saturation-dominated absolute
numbers — the relative comparison is what the thesis needs.

### F1+ — Thesis main claim at 10× scale (§6.5.0a, n=19k full Azure)
![fig_f_full19k_gpu_vs_hybrid](figures/fig_f_full19k_gpu_vs_hybrid.png)

Direct apples-to-apples confirmation of F1 at the full 19,365-row
Azure conv trace. Same hybrid-wins-both-models pattern. **Pi0**: +63%
throughput, −53% E2E p99. **OpenVLA**: +29% throughput, **−26% E2E p99
(2× stronger than at n=2k)**.

The OpenVLA hybrid advantage *grew* with scale because GPU-only at 10×
the request density experiences more queueing pressure, amplifying
the relative benefit of PIM offload. Pi0's gap stayed roughly
constant because Pi0's per-request decode cost is already small and
queueing dynamics matter less.

Side-by-side:

| Metric | E5 v2 (n=2k) | F (n=19k) |
|---|---|---|
| Pi0 throughput gain | +59% | **+63%** |
| Pi0 E2E p99 reduction | −52% | **−53%** |
| OpenVLA throughput gain | +26% | **+29%** |
| OpenVLA E2E p99 reduction | −13% | **−26%** ← 2× stronger |

Route mix: 99.88% PIM for Pi0, 99.87% PIM for OpenVLA (same as E5 v2).
Wall time: 32k s for cell 01 (Pi0 hybrid, cold cache) → ~9h; the
other 3 cells were 1.5–4 h each (warm cache). All 4 cells finished
without intervention after the `--no-kv-timeline` flag landed.

### F2 — Batch size scaling (§6.6)
![fig_e6_bs_throughput](figures/fig_e6_bs_throughput.png)

The "1/B regression" from earlier synthetic-VLA runs is **not real on
Azure conv**. Throughput scales 0.66→3.36 req/s for Pi0 (5.1×) and
0.21→1.11 for OpenVLA (5.3×) over bs=1→8. Then a starvation cliff at
bs=16: mean_bs collapses to 1.30 / 1.06 because the decode scheduler
can't fill cohorts of 16 at max_active=32.

### F8 — KV allocation policy comparison (§6.2)
![fig_kv_policy_compare](figures/fig_kv_policy_compare.png)

Three policies head-to-head on the **moderate-load regime** where the
policy choice actually matters — OpenVLA, Azure conv, n=2000,
arrival_scale=3.0, kv_pool=4 GiB, max_active=32, SLO=500ms
(`sweep_openvla_n2000_20260513_161108/05_slo500_max_util_full/` etc).

| Metric | guaranteed_no_evict | max_util_full | max_util_tail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Throughput | 0.96 req/s | **1.57 req/s** ★ | 1.57 req/s |
| E2E p99 | 1 611 390 ms | **111 419 ms** ★ | 128 450 ms |
| Admitted | 2 000 | **2 289** ★ | 2 123 |
| Tokens recomputed | **0** ★ | 16 832 | 63 273 |

Story: **`max_util_full` wins on every user-facing metric.** Chunked
admission + full-block eviction keeps the KV pool unblocked and admits
+14% more requests than gne, with throughput +63% and E2E p99 14×
lower (1.6M → 111k ms). `max_util_tail` matches throughput but fine-
grained tail-trim incurs ~10× more preemptions (2 933 vs 289) and
recomputes 3.8× more tokens, dragging E2E p99 ~15% worse than full.
gne trivially "wins" recomputation (zero preemption = zero recomp) but
its conservative admission leaves the pool half-empty under load, so
TTFT and E2E p99 explode into the minutes range.

This is why the thesis uses **`max_util_full + max_consecutive_decode_batches=4`**
as the operating point everywhere downstream of §6.2 (E5 main claim,
E6 batching, F full-19k).

> Note: under **deep saturation** (scale=1.0, e.g. E5), the three
> policies converge — the system is admission-bound regardless of
> eviction strategy and the differences collapse. The story above is
> the regime that decided the default.

### F9 — Peak-RPS knee curve (§6.1)
![fig_rps_knee](figures/fig_rps_knee.png)

The knee marks the **peak RPS `a`** our system can sustain at p99-SLO:
beyond `a`, E2E p99 rises super-linearly. Three lines (one per
dataset), log-log axes, star annotation marks each dataset's `a`.

Measured values (Pi0, max_util_full, 8 GiB pool, bs=4, max_active=32,
A6000, sweep `rps_knee_pi0_20260529_224452`, 2026-05-30):

| Dataset | throughput ceiling | knee detected at | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `vla_poisson` | **23.4 req/s** | **a ≈ 20.1 req/s** (scale=1.5, p99=943ms) | Short Lin (306) + short Lout (7); under SLO at offered ≤12 RPS. |
| `azure_poisson` | 2.63 req/s | **a ≈ 2.24 req/s** (scale=2.5, p99=88s) | Knee is the last point BEFORE the throughput ceiling; saturation cluster at 2.63 holds 7 cells. |
| `azure_empirical` | 2.53 req/s | **a ≈ 2.23 req/s** (scale=2.5, p99=222s) | Essentially identical knee to Poisson — shape variance doesn't move the elbow. |

**Headline findings**:

1. **9× gap between robotic and conversational** workloads on the
   same hardware (20.1 / 2.23). Dominated by mean Lout (7 vs 211) —
   decode steps per request drive everything.
2. **Heavy-tail Azure_empirical knees at the same RPS as Poisson**
   (2.23 vs 2.24). Shape variance moves the throughput ceiling
   slightly (~4%, 2.53 vs 2.63) but not the elbow itself. Practical
   implication: `azure_poisson` is a fine stand-in for the real
   Azure shape when measuring knees.
3. **Azure on Pi0 violates the 500ms SLO at every tested rate**.
   Even at 0.56 RPS, p99 = 8.6s. The SLO is fundamentally
   incompatible with Pi0's per-request decode time on Azure-length
   contexts. This is why the thesis comparison uses *relative*
   metrics (hybrid vs gpu-only) rather than absolute SLO compliance.

Methodology: see [docs/plan/rps_knee_finder.md](../docs/plan/rps_knee_finder.md).
Per-dataset figures F9a/b/c follow this combined view — each isolates
one workload for closer inspection of its own knee.

> ⚠️ **`a` is config-dependent.** The numbers in the table are for
> `(model=Pi0, mode=hybrid, policy=max_util_full, kv_pool=8GiB, bs=4)`.
> Different models or knobs shift the knee — OpenVLA at the Pi0-derived
> scale is already deep in saturation, so its own `a` is substantially
> lower. A separate OpenVLA knee-finder run is needed to characterize
> it; see [docs/plan/knee_scaled_experiments.md](../docs/plan/knee_scaled_experiments.md).

**Linear-axes variants (F9-lin, F9a-lin, …)**: same data with linear
RPS × p99 axes for an intuitive read of the elbow shape. The combined
view becomes a 3-panel layout (one subplot per dataset) because RPS
ranges differ ~10× across datasets and a single linear panel would
crush Azure into the leftmost sliver. The y-axis is auto-capped at
2.5–3× the knee p99 so the saturation cluster doesn't dominate. Use
`--linear` on `thesis_rps_knee.py` to regenerate.

### F12 — RPS-over-time before vs after scaling to `a` (§6.1)

Two-panel figures showing each trace's instantaneous RPS **before**
scaling (arrival_scale=1.0) and **after** scaling so the peak
instantaneous RPS touches `a`. Bars are colored by their relation to
`a`: green = under, amber = near, red = over.

by the same factor as the timestamps so both panels render the same
number of bars (e.g. azure_full19k: top = 5 s buckets, bottom =
21.2 s buckets). The y-axis label shows the bucket size explicitly.
Without this fix the bottom panel's bars would be `scale×` thinner
because the time-axis extent grows proportionally.

|---|---|---|---|
| `vla_poisson` | 49.0 RPS | ÷2.44 (slow down) | 20.1 |
| `azure_poisson` | 8.4 RPS | ÷3.19 (rendered) or ÷3.75 with knee a=2.24 | 2.24 (knee) / 2.63 (ceiling) |
| `azure_empirical` | 8.4 RPS | ÷3.32 (rendered) or ÷3.77 with knee a=2.23 | 2.23 (knee) / 2.53 (ceiling) |
| **`azure_full19k`** (real Azure) | 10.6 RPS | ÷4.24 (rendered) — placeholder until full19k sweep | 2.5 (placeholder) |

The F12 figures are currently rendered with the previous ceiling-based `a` (2.63 / 2.53). After Phase 2's saturation-aware knee detection, the **true elbow** is at 2.24 / 2.23 — re-render with `--a-rps 2.24` (or `2.23`) for the knee-correct version.

**Why both VLA and Azure need slowing down**: each preset was
generated at a rate (30 Hz, 5.5 Hz) chosen so that **the base trace
already saturates the system**. The "scaled" panel shows the regime
where the simulator can actually serve all requests at their SLO —
green dominates, with occasional amber/red excursions where the
trace's bursty Poisson arrivals briefly touch `a`. That's exactly the
operating point we want for the thesis main claim.

The scale factor IS the `--arrival-scale` you would pass to the
runner to put the trace at the knee. The bottom-panel labels show
exactly this number.
